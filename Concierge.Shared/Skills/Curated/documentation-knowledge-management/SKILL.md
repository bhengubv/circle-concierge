# Documentation and Knowledge Management

Use this skill when the user needs durable knowledge: docs, SOPs, knowledge bases, decision records, onboarding material, runbooks, research notes, or Obsidian-style connected thinking.

## Workflow

1. Identify audience, purpose, lifecycle, source evidence, owner, update cadence, and retrieval path.
2. Choose documentation type: quickstart, how-to, reference, concept, SOP, runbook, FAQ, ADR, decision log, glossary, or knowledge map.
3. Structure information for reuse: titles, summaries, tags, links, prerequisites, steps, examples, warnings, owners, and last-reviewed dates.
4. Connect knowledge across products, departments, projects, clients, entities, and evidence bundles.
5. Add maintenance rules so docs stay alive instead of becoming stale artifacts.

## Coverage

- Product docs, API docs, internal SOPs, support knowledge bases, onboarding, runbooks, release notes, and governance records.
- Obsidian-style backlinks, maps of content, indexes, tags, decision trails, research synthesis, and knowledge graphs.
- Docs-as-code, versioning, templates, searchability, ownership, review cycles, and content quality.

## Guardrails

- Do not document guesses as facts.
- Mark stale, unverified, sensitive, confidential, or regulated information clearly.
- Protect secrets, personal data, customer data, legal material, and private strategy notes.

## Output

Produce documentation plans, SOPs, knowledge maps, templates, decision records, runbooks, indexes, maintenance rules, and evidence-linked summaries.
